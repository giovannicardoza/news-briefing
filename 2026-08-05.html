MMarkets and Economy

  • The Fed held rates at 3.50%-3.75% with three dissents favoring a hike, and stocks sold off in response (Dow -1.5%, S&P and Nasdaq -0.6%): markets now price a 60% chance of a September hike.
  • Q2 GDP grew just 1.5% versus 2.3% expected, weaker growth that complicates the Fed's calculus by reducing the case for further tightening even as inflation stays elevated.
  • Core inflation remains above target, driven partly by energy price shocks from the Iran conflict: a supply-side pressure that argues for a hawkish Fed independent of demand.

GGeopolitics

  • The US-Iran ceasefire reopened the Strait of Hormuz and eased nuclear sanctions, removing a major risk premium from oil prices and reopening Iran's economy to trade.
  • The US-Canada trade war escalated to 25% tariffs on goods and 10% on energy: Canada supplies 40% of US crude and critical minerals, so the tariffs raise US fuel and manufacturing costs directly.
  • After the Supreme Court struck down the administration's IEEPA tariff authority in February, it pivoted to Section 232 duties of 25-50% on steel, aluminum, semiconductors, and autos, now costing the average US household about $900 this year.

TTech and AI

  • Trump signed an AI Innovation and Security executive order building a federal AI security framework, signaling Washington favors managed growth over restriction.
  • California's AI Transparency Act took effect August 2, the first major state AI disclosure law, likely to become a template other states copy.
  • Nvidia, Microsoft, and Meta are lobbying against "premature restrictions" on open-weight models, while the Magnificent Seven now make up 35% of the S&P 500, concentrating market risk in a handful of AI bets.

EEnergy and Commodities

  • WTI crude sits near $80/barrel after the Strait of Hormuz reopened, with supply on track to return to pre-conflict levels by year-end and JPMorgan projecting Brent near $78 by Q4.
  • Gasoline is forecast to fall to $3.80/gallon in Q3 from over $4.20 in Q2, passing the oil price relief through to consumers at the pump.
  • Natural gas trades near $2.69/MMBtu on record US production of 110.7 Bcf/d, keeping summer electricity costs below 2025 levels despite peak cooling demand.
