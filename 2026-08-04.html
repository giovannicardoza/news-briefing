MMarkets and Economy

  • The Fed held rates at 3.50-3.75% with three members dissenting in favor of hikes, and markets have fully repriced from rate cuts to 1-2 hikes by year end as inflation stays above target.
  • Equities sold off after the decision, with the Dow down 1.5% and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq down 0.6%, as hawkish Fed messaging tightens financial conditions.
  • This week's JOLTS report and Friday's July jobs data are the next major catalysts: a soft print could reopen the door to a pause despite the Fed's hawkish tilt.

GGeopolitics

  • A June 2026 US-Iran memorandum restored shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a corridor for roughly 30% of seaborne oil, easing the acute supply risk that had pushed crude toward $90 in recent weeks.
  • G7 tightened sanctions on Russian oil and gas exports amid a stalled Ukraine war, and Washington ordered a blockade on Venezuelan oil tankers, pulling roughly 700,000 barrels a day of supply even as the Hormuz corridor reopens.
  • The Supreme Court struck down the administration's IEEPA tariff authority, and Washington pivoted to a 10% Section 122 blanket surcharge plus Section 232/301 tools, an estimated $900 a year in added costs per household.
  • The US-Canada trade war escalated further, with tariffs on most Canadian goods and energy met by $155 billion in Canadian counter-tariffs on autos, energy, and agricultural inputs.

TTech and AI

  • The EU AI Act's core transparency rules took effect August 2, requiring disclosure of AI interactions and labeled deepfakes, though deadlines for high-risk uses like hiring and credit scoring were pushed to December 2027.
  • Seven US states enacted their own AI laws while federal preemption stalled in the House, layering a state-by-state compliance patchwork on top of the new EU rules for companies operating AI at scale.
  • Palantir's Q2 revenue jumped 93% year over year to $1.94 billion on 149% growth in US commercial revenue, and Amazon's and Alphabet's AI stakes added more than $45 billion combined to their balance sheets, both signs enterprise AI demand keeps outrunning expectations already priced into valuations.
  • Antitrust regulators are widening their lens on AI deals to scrutinize talent-and-licensing arrangements, not just traditional market-share concentration.

EEnergy and Commodities

  • Crude fell sharply on August 4, with WTI down 4.58% to $76.66 and Brent down 5.69% to $82.92, as OPEC supply increases and the Hormuz de-escalation outweighed the Russia and Venezuela supply losses.
  • JPMorgan sees Brent easing further to $80 in Q4 and $78 by year end, a forecast that would help take pressure off inflation if it holds.
  • Natural gas fell to $2.77/MMBtu, down nearly 15% in a month, on record Lower 48 production and inventories running above the seasonal average.
  • The EIA expects wholesale electricity prices to come in lower than last summer thanks to cheap natural gas, even as power-sector demand rises to 42.2 Bcf/d.

WWildcard

  • Andy Burnham replaced Keir Starmer as UK Prime Minister, a leadership change in a G7 economy worth watching for shifts in trade and fiscal posture.
  • The Bank for International Settlements warned that US equity valuations sit near the second-highest cyclically adjusted P/E ratio on record, a setup where any correction could carry outsized macro consequences.
